Case A · the outside

The Seller's Claim

Reuven sells his field to Shimon and later claims the sale was conditional — "I only agreed to sell on the condition you'd return it within 30 days." Shimon says no such condition was made. The money has already changed hands. Who do we believe?

The Talmudic move · INFERENCE
Reuven could have simply denied the sale ever happened — a stronger claim. Since he admits the sale but makes the weaker, conditional claim, we believe him. The fact that a more sweeping denial was available supports the narrower one.
The formula
מִגּוֹ
"miggo" — since he could have said something stronger, believe the weaker claim.

Case B · the outside

The Conditional Get

A man sends a get (divorce document) to his wife via an agent, adding: "It is valid only if I haven't cancelled it before it reaches her." The wife receives the get. The husband claims he cancelled it verbally — the wife says she heard nothing. Is she free to remarry?

The same Talmudic move · INFERENCE
The husband could have claimed he never sent any get at all — a stronger denial. Since he admits the get but raises the weaker claim of verbal cancellation, the miggo runs against him: conceding the get undercuts his cancellation claim. She is believed.
